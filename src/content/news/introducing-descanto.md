Every AI agent demo ends the same way: the agent is brilliant for ninety seconds, and then you wouldn't trust it with your inbox. The gap isn't intelligence — it's structure. Real coworkers have accountability. They have their own desk, their own computer, a record of what they did, and they ask before doing something irreversible.

Descanto is our attempt to build that structure — from both ends.

## Botto: the teammate

Botto gives you a team of Bots with names, jobs, and memory. You message them like coworkers. They run on your machine, think with the AI subscription you already pay for, and every action lands in a transcript that doubles as the audit log. It's open source under MIT — daemon to desktop — and free.

## Canto: the computer

Every Botto Bot gets its own persistent cloud desktop, and that infrastructure is a product in its own right. Canto desktops wake in half a second with tabs, processes, and logins intact. They fork twenty ways in milliseconds for parallel runs. And they meter honestly: billing runs only while your machine responds — our slowness can never charge you. Canto enters early access today, with Botto as customer zero.

If you've wanted to hand real work to an agent — and actually go do something else — this is for you. Download Botto, or join the Canto waitlist. And if you'd rather read the code than the pitch: the repo is the front door.
